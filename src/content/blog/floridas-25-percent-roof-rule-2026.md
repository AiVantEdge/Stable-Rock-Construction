If part of your roof is damaged, one Florida rule decides whether you can patch it or have to replace the whole thing — the **25% rule**. It's about to change, and most homeowners (and a lot of contractors) are still working off the old version.

## What the 25% rule says today

Under the Florida Building Code, if **more than 25% of a roof section is repaired or replaced within any 12-month period**, that entire section generally has to be brought up to *current* code — not just patched back to how it was. On an older South Florida roof, that often turns a "big repair" into a full replacement, because current code means current HVHZ standards, fastening, and underlayment.

## What's changing with the 9th Edition Building Code

The 9th Edition of the Florida Building Code (taking effect at the **end of 2026**) revises the rule so it's less punishing for newer roofs. The key change: if the **undamaged portion of your roof was already built to the 2007 Florida Building Code or later**, you may be allowed to repair the damaged area **without** re-doing the whole roof, even if the damage is over 25%.

Older roofs (built before that 2007-code era) still fall under the stricter "replace the whole section" version of the rule.

> The practical takeaway: newer roofs get more flexibility to repair; older roofs still often trigger a full replacement once damage crosses 25%.

## Why this matters for your wallet

Two things decide whether you're facing a repair or a replacement:

1. **How much of the roof is affected** (the 25% threshold), and
2. **How old the roof is / what code it was built to.**

There's also an insurance angle: many Florida policies include **law-and-ordinance coverage**, which can help pay for the code-required upgrades when the 25% rule forces a bigger job. It's worth checking your policy before you assume a repair is all you'll get.
