Sun, heat, salt air, and summer downpours are hard on a roof. In South Florida, the honest answer to "how long will my roof last" depends almost entirely on the system you have and whether it was installed to code in the first place.

## Typical roof lifespans in Miami and the Keys

- **Flat and low-slope systems (built-up, modified bitumen / torch-down):** about 15 to 25 years. Ponding water and UV exposure are what wear them out first.
- **Concrete and clay tile:** the tile itself can last 40+ years, but the underlayment beneath it usually fails around the 20-year mark, which is what actually causes leaks.
- **Metal (standing seam):** 40 years or more, and it stands up to wind better than most alternatives, which is why we install a lot of it down here.
- **Shingle:** shorter in this climate than up north — often 15 to 20 years — because heat and storms accelerate wear.

## Repair or replace?

A single leak or a few loose tiles is usually a repair. But if you are seeing ponding water, widespread blistering, granules washing out, or leaks at multiple seams, you are past the point where patching pays off. Under the [Florida Building Code](/roofing), if more than 25% of a roof is replaced within 12 months, that section generally has to be brought up to current code anyway — so a series of "cheap" repairs can quietly turn into a full re-roof.

## What makes a roof last longer here

1. **Correct installation to HVHZ code.** Products need a Miami-Dade Notice of Acceptance (NOA), and the fastening and flashing details are what actually keep water out in a storm.
2. **Drainage.** Flat roofs fail at the low spots. Proper slope and working scuppers matter more than the membrane brand.
3. **Maintenance.** Clearing debris and catching a small flashing issue early is the cheapest roof work you will ever do.
