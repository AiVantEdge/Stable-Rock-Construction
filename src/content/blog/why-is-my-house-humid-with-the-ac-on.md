Your A/C is running, the thermostat says 74, and the house *still* feels clammy. In South Florida that's one of the most common comfort complaints — and it's almost never about temperature. It's about **humidity**, and a few specific things cause it.

## 1. The system is oversized

This is the big one. An A/C that's too large for the house cools the air fast, then shuts off — before it has run long enough to pull the moisture out. That's called **short-cycling**, and it leaves you with a cold, damp house. Bigger is not better down here. The fix starts with a proper **Manual J load calculation** so the system is sized to the house, not guessed from square footage.

## 2. The fan is set to ON instead of AUTO

Quick, free thing to check right now. If your thermostat fan is set to **ON**, the blower runs constantly — including when the compressor is off — and it re-evaporates the moisture sitting on the coil back into your house. Switch it to **AUTO** so the fan only runs while the system is actively cooling and dehumidifying.

## 3. Leaky return ducts

If your return ductwork leaks, it pulls hot, humid air straight out of the attic and mixes it into your supply. You pay to cool attic air, and the humidity never drops. Sealing or replacing bad ducts often does more for comfort than a new unit would.

## 4. It's genuinely just very humid

Sometimes the equipment is fine and the load is just extreme — a lot of glass, a pool, high occupancy. In those cases a **variable-speed system** or a **whole-home dehumidifier** is the real answer, because it can run long, low, and dry without overcooling.
